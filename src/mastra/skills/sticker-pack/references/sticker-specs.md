# Sticker sheet spec

What separates a sticker sheet from a collage grid, and the details that
make each cutout read as a real sticker rather than a cropped photo.

## What makes this different from doodle-collage

`doodle-collage` produces panels that share one bordered grid — the frame
lines are part of the composition. A sticker sheet is the opposite: every
sticker is a **standalone object** with its own die-cut edge, floating on a
plain sheet background, meant to be imagined as peeled apart individually.
If the output reads as boxed-in panels, it drifted into collage territory.

## Per-sticker requirements

| Element        | Spec                                                                 |
| -------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Crop           | Head and shoulders bust, not full body                                |
| Border         | Even-thickness white die-cut outline tightly hugging the silhouette   |
| Surface        | Very subtle paper-grain texture — visible but not distracting         |
| Shadow         | Short, soft drop shadow suggesting a physical peel-off sticker        |
| Background     | Plain warm-white or neutral sheet, no props or scenery                |

## Count and arrangement

Four or five stickers per sheet by default. Arrange them loosely — a 2x2
grid with generous gaps, a relaxed cluster with slight overlap at corners,
or a single row at playful tilt angles. Avoid ruler-straight rows; a little
looseness is what sells the "real sticker sheet" feeling.

## Pose variety

Keep the character identical (hairstyle, face shape, skin tone, outfit)
across every sticker, and vary only the expression and small gesture:

- a cheerful wave
- a wink with a small smile
- a big open-mouth laugh
- a shy peace sign
- a thumbs up
- a blown kiss

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Drawing one big scene and calling it a sticker sheet (no individual
  borders/shadows) — that is a collage, not this skill.
- Cropping to full body — stickers here are bust-only.
- Overlapping die-cut borders between stickers.
- Adding text or a logo onto the sheet itself.
