# Bad on Purpose: The Line Between Charming and Broken

The goal is a genuine 4-year-old's crayon drawing — clumsy with love, not
glitchy with AI failure. Every choice should make you smile at the kid's
effort, never wince at something uncanny.

## Do / Don't pairs

| Charming-bad (DO) | Broken-bad (DO NOT) |
|---|---|
| Wobbly, uneven outlines that wander | Melted, dripping, or dissolving anatomy |
| Colour scribbled enthusiastically outside the lines | Smeared digital artifacts or blended noise |
| Wrong proportions (huge head, stick limbs, tiny torso) | Distorted limbs at impossible angles, extra fingers |
| Lopsided smile, mismatched eyes (one bigger) | Horror-uncanny expressions, hollow eyes, missing face |
| Misspelled name in shaky child handwriting | Garbled AI text, random Unicode, unreadable glyphs |
| Simple circle-and-lines sun in the corner | Photorealistic environment composited behind a sketch |
| Flat waxy crayon texture on paper | Glossy 3D rendering, realistic lighting or reflections |
| Two dots for a nose, no nostrils drawn | Hyper-detailed realistic nose on a stick-figure body |
| Stick-figure fingers (or mitten hands) | Body horror: fused fingers, too many joints |
| A wobbly drawn heart or star nearby | AI hallucination objects floating without context |

## Recognisability anchors to preserve

Even at maximum childlike distortion, the subject must still be
identifiable. Preserve:

1. **Hair colour and rough silhouette** — a kid draws "brown curly hair" as
   a scribbled brown cloud on top of the circle head. That is enough.
2. **Glasses** — if the person wears them, draw two wonky circles on the
   face. A child always notices glasses.
3. **One signature feature** — beard, freckles, a hat, a bright shirt
   colour, a distinctive accessory. Pick whichever is most prominent.

Everything else can (and should) be wildly wrong: wrong number of fingers,
body too small, feet pointing the wrong way, arms coming from the head.

## The texture test

If you removed the person's features and just looked at the *medium*, it
should read as:

- Waxy crayon strokes on slightly textured white or lined paper
- Uneven pressure (darker where the kid pressed hard)
- Visible paper grain showing through lighter strokes
- Maybe a few stray scribble marks where the kid changed their mind

It should NOT read as:

- A Photoshop filter applied to a photograph
- A clean vector illustration with a "crayon brush" overlay
- Digital watercolour or marker rendering
- Any medium that looks professional or intentional
