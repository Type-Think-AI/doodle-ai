# Chibi Mini-Me Overlay — Recipe Anatomy

Reference: @Ciri_ai 1 May 2026 (3.5k likes, 224k views, 2.3k bookmarks).
Chinese/Xiaohongshu name: Q版 (Q-version chibi overlay).

## What makes it work

The trick is **contrast of register** — a photographic base layer (real
lighting, real skin texture, real depth of field) versus obviously
hand-drawn cartoon elements on top. The brain instantly reads the chibis as
imaginary and the photo as real, which makes it endearing without being
uncanny. Destroying the photographic base (cartoonifying the whole image)
kills the magic and turns it into just another avatar.

## Anatomy of the overlay

### Base layer (untouched photo)

- Must remain fully photographic: no posterize, no paint filter, no
  skin-smooth, no colour-grade shift.
- Subject's face and body are never retouched or distorted.
- The photo provides all the lighting and depth; the overlay is flat by
  design.

### Mini-chibi placement zones

Think of the subject as centre-stage with zones radiating outward:

| Zone | Location | Good for |
|------|----------|----------|
| **Shoulder perch** | On or near the subject's shoulder/head | climbing, sitting, whispering |
| **Lower corners** | Bottom-left / bottom-right margins | cheering, holding signs, napping |
| **Opposite side** | Across from the subject's facing direction | waving, mirroring the pose |
| **Floating** | Above or beside the subject's head | thought-bubble reactions, holding sparkles |

**Rules:**
- Never place a chibi over the subject's face.
- Never cluster all chibis in one zone — distribute for balance.
- 3–5 chibis is the sweet spot; more than 5 becomes busy.

### Chibi style spec

- Head-to-body ratio ≈ 1:1.5 (big head, tiny body — Q-version proportions).
- Bold uniform-weight outline (≈2–3 px at 1024 px canvas).
- Flat fills, no shading gradients, no highlights.
- Recognizable as the same person: match hair colour/style, outfit colour,
  glasses if present. Simplify details, keep silhouette identity.
- Each chibi performs a **distinct small action** related to the photo's
  context.

### Handwritten captions and arrows

- Marker-style lettering: slightly uneven baseline, thick strokes, 2–4
  words max per caption.
- Arrows are hand-drawn (wobbly, not Bézier-smooth) pointing from the
  caption to its target.
- Place captions in negative space — never overlapping the subject's face
  or a chibi's face.
- Limit to 2–3 captions total; more clutters the composition.

### Doodle accents

- Sparkles (4-pointed star bursts), small hearts, short motion lines.
- Scattered lightly, concentrated near chibis or caption endpoints.
- Same line weight as the chibi outlines for visual coherence.
- Never so dense that they obscure the photo.

## Density limits (what makes it cluttered)

| Element | Max per image | Symptom if exceeded |
|---------|--------------|---------------------|
| Chibis | 5 | Photo becomes unreadable |
| Captions | 3 | Looks like a comic page, not a selfie |
| Accents (sparkles/hearts) | ~8–12 individual marks | "Sticker bomb" noise |

## Authentic vs cheap

| Authentic | Cheap |
|-----------|-------|
| Photo is crisp and untouched | Photo is blurred, filtered, or posterized |
| Chibis match the actual person | Generic anime faces unrelated to subject |
| Captions react to photo context | Random motivational quotes |
| Balanced placement with breathing room | Every inch covered in doodles |
| Uniform line weight, one colour palette | Mixed clip-art styles pasted on |
| Hand-drawn arrow style | Straight vector arrows or speech bubbles |
