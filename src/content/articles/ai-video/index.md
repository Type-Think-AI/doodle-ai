**Direct answer:** Doodle AI can turn your doodle into a **short animated clip — 5 to 15 seconds long, with sound** — at [doodleai.art](https://doodleai.art). You choose the length, and it costs **1 credit per second** (a 5-second clip is 5 credits). New accounts get 10 free credits on signup, and a clip that fails to generate refunds automatically. You can animate a doodle you already have, make a fresh clip starring your doodle character, or describe a clip in words with no picture at all.

Everywhere else on this site turns a photo into a still doodle. This page is about the moving version: taking that hand-drawn character and giving it a few seconds of life, complete with audio.

## What an animated doodle clip is

It's a short video — as little as 5 seconds, as long as 15 — of your doodle in motion. It arrives **with sound already in it**; you don't record or attach audio yourself. Think of it as the difference between a drawing of your character and a tiny scene of them: a wave, a blink, a little moment.

You set the length before generating. Shorter is cheaper and faster; longer gives the clip more room to play out.

## What it costs and how long it takes

Video is priced by the second: **1 credit for every second of finished clip.** So a 5-second clip is 5 credits and a 15-second clip is 15. A still doodle image, by comparison, is 1 credit flat — a clip costs more because there's a lot more to render. Your 10 free signup credits are enough to try a couple of short clips.

Generation usually finishes in under a couple of minutes for a short clip; a longer one under heavy load can take a few minutes more. You don't have to sit and watch — the clip shows up when it's ready. If something goes wrong on the AI's side, the credits go straight back to your balance.

## Animate your doodle vs. make a new clip of your character

This is the one choice worth understanding, because the two look similar but do different things:

- **Animate your doodle** — the clip *starts from the exact picture you have* and brings it to life. The doodle you see is the first frame, and it moves from there. Use this when you want *that specific image* to move.
- **Make a new clip of your character** — the AI keeps *who your character is* (their look, their identity) but puts them in a **new scene** that doesn't begin from your picture. Use this when you want a fresh moment starring the same character, not the original frame moving.

A quick way to remember it: *animate* takes your picture and moves it; *new clip* takes your character and gives them a new little scene.

You can also skip the picture entirely and just **describe the clip in words** — the AI generates it from your prompt alone.
