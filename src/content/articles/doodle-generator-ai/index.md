**Direct answer:** The AI doodle generator at [doodleai.art](https://doodleai.art) uses an illustration model to redraw your photo as a doodle. Each AI generation costs 1 credit and refunds on failure; new accounts get free starter credits.

The "AI" part matters because it changes what you get. A filter maps effects onto pixels and stops. A generative model *reinterprets* the photo — deciding how to simplify a jawline into two strokes, how to flatten hair into a shape — which is why the result reads as a hand-drawn doodle instead of a processed photo.

## Why AI, not a filter

Filters are deterministic and shallow. AI illustration is interpretive: it understands "this is a face, these are glasses, this is a smile" and re-renders them in the doodle style. The cost of that flexibility is variability — two runs can differ — but it is what makes the drawing feel alive.

## Steering the model

Feed the AI a clear, well-lit photo of one subject and keep the prompt tight:

> Doodle of me. Keep the buzz cut and the earrings. Flat color, no text, warm-white background.

Naming two or three landmarks gives the model something concrete to preserve.
