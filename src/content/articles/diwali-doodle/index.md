**Direct answer:** To make a Diwali doodle at [doodleai.art](https://doodleai.art), sign in, attach your photo, and run the [Festival skill](https://doodleai.art/skills/festival/) with "Diwali" in your prompt. You get a festive hand-drawn portrait of yourself — diyas, string lights, and rangoli-style motifs around your own doodle. It does **not** draw deities or religious icons. Each run is 1 credit and refunds on failure; new accounts get free starter credits.

"Diwali doodle" often surfaces the famous 2008 Google Doodle in search. This is a different thing: a festive doodle *of you*, made from your photo, that you can send as a greeting or set as a display picture during the festival of lights.

## A festive portrait, not deity art

The [Festival skill](https://doodleai.art/skills/festival/) redraws **your photo** into a hand-drawn festive portrait. For Diwali it adds decorative festival elements — lamps, warm lights, festive color, rangoli-style patterns — around your own likeness. It keeps your face, hair, and expression recognizable. It is intentionally a portrait of a person, not an image of any deity, temple icon, or religious figure.

## How to make one

Type "Diwali" plus a short note in the box above, sign in with Google, attach a clear photo, and generate. A starter prompt:

> Make a festive Diwali doodle from my photo. Keep my hair and outfit. Warm diyas and string lights around me, no text, no deities.

One credit per run, refunded on failure. Works from a phone browser.
